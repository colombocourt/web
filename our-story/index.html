Colombo Court / Our Story

A hotel built from
what was already here.

Old office buildings in Colombo 3, converted into a hotel with salvaged brick, reclaimed timber and a tree of scrap metal rising through the atrium.

Read our story See the rooms

One

It began with old office buildings.

Colombo Court stands on Alfred House Avenue, on a site that was once occupied by old office buildings. Many owners would have demolished them and built something new.

Instead, the buildings were kept and converted into a hotel. This decision shaped much of what guests see today. The rooms have different layouts because they follow the original structure. A garden with a lap pool sits at the centre, and the rooms look on to it.

  • Old office buildings, converted into a hotel
  • Reused rather than demolished
  • 1 poolside garden at the centre
  • 31 rooms, each with its own layout

Two

Designed by architect Sunela Jayewardene

Sunela Jayewardene is widely recognised as one of Sri Lanka’s leading environmental architects. Her award-winning hotel designs include Jetwing Vil Uyana and Rainforest Ecolodge. She was the first Chairperson of the Federation of Environmental Organizations of Sri Lanka. She is also an author: her book The Line of Lanka was nominated for the 2017 Gratiaen Prize, and her second book is Ravana’s Lanka.

Her design responds to Sri Lanka’s heat, rain and sunlight. The rooms face inward on to a garden planted with mature trees. The trees give shade, air moves through the open space, and the rooms look on to greenery instead of the street. This keeps the hotel quiet and helps the building stay cool.

The most sustainable building is often the one that already exists.

Three

Materials with a second life.

Salvaged brick in the walls, reclaimed timber, and bar stools in the Loft made from old tractor seats.

  • Salvaged brick

    The walls are built from salvaged brick. Reusing brick saves the clay, firing and transport that new brick would need.

  • Reclaimed timber

    The timber in the ceilings and doors was reclaimed from other buildings instead of being cut from new trees. Some of it is much older than the hotel.

  • Tractor seats at the Loft

    The stools at the counter in the Loft bar are made from cast tractor seats fitted on new legs.

Four

A tree made from scrap metal.

The sculptures around the hotel are by Prageeth Manohansa, a Sri Lankan artist who works mainly with discarded metal. The largest is a tree that rises through the atrium towards the glass roof.

Its branches are made from metal offcuts, and its birds and leaves from scrap and wire that would otherwise have been thrown away. Like the building itself, it gives old materials a new purpose.

Five

The first CarbonNeutral city hotel in South Asia.

Colombo Court is South Asia’s first city hotel certified CarbonNeutral®. The hotel measures its carbon footprint, reduces it wherever possible, and offsets the rest.

Much of this work is part of daily life at the hotel. Drinking water is filtered and bottled in glass on site. Water is recycled for the garden. Waste is separated before it leaves the hotel. Produce is bought locally wherever possible.

  • Certified CarbonNeutral®
  • 1st city hotel in South Asia to achieve it
  • Glass bottles for drinking water
  • Local produce, wherever possible

Colombo Court has also been named Luxury Green Hotel, Indian Ocean, at the World Luxury Hotel Awards.

Six

Thirty-one rooms and a team who know you.

In a hotel of this size, you see the same friendly faces throughout your stay. The team who welcome you soon learn how you like your coffee. The chefs who cook for an evening event may also have prepared breakfast that morning. If you need anything, the person you speak to can usually help straight away.

That personal service is at the heart of Colombo Court. We have thirty-one rooms in six categories, each with its own layout, around a peaceful poolside garden. We look forward to welcoming you.
